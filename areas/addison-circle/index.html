Water Damage Restoration in Addison Circle, Addison, TX

Water in a stacked, shared building moves in ways a single house never does. Here is how to sort out who handles what and how drying works.

☎ (972) 945-2885

Addison Circle is a walkable, mixed-use part of town beside the Dallas North Tollway, with apartments, condos and townhomes sitting close to office and retail space. When water shows up in a building like that, the first question is rarely just how to dry it. It is also whose pipe, whose ceiling and whose responsibility.

Call, say which unit or space is wet, and ask about availability. While you wait, stop the water if you safely can, tell your building management or association, and take photos before anything is moved.

Leaks that travel between units

In a stacked building, a supply line that fails on the third floor can show up as a stain on the ceiling below. Water follows framing, pipe chases and electrical penetrations, so the wet spot may be a few feet from the source.

Shared walls add another layer. A neighbor's dishwasher hose or water heater closet can soak drywall that belongs to your unit. A moisture meter and thermal camera help a restoration team trace the wet path. Water landing in a shop or office below is covered under commercial water damage.

Ceilings and the wall cavity behind them

A ceiling that sags or bubbles is holding water. Do not poke it open over your head. A restoration team opens it in a controlled way and dries the cavity above. See drywall water damage.

Water safety steps before you do anything else

Water, ceilings and outlets do not mix.

  • Shut off the nearest supply valve, such as the one under a sink or behind a toilet or water heater.
  • If you cannot stop the flow, call the Town of Addison to shut water off at the meter. Only an authorized agent may do this, so do not wrench the meter yourself.
  • Stay away from wet ceilings with light fixtures. If water is dripping through a fixture, leave the switch off and call an electrician.
  • Do not use a wet outlet or touch a plugged-in device standing in water.
  • If you smell gas, leave the unit and call the gas utility or 911 from outside.
  • Call 911 for any immediate danger to people, such as a ceiling collapse or electrical sparking.

What drying a shared-wall unit involves

The goal is to remove standing water, dry the structure and take out only what cannot be saved. Industry practice follows ANSI/IICRC S500.

  1. Map the wet area. Moisture meters and a thermal camera show how far water traveled, including into walls shared with a neighbor.
  2. Extract standing water. Portable extractors lift water from carpet, pad and hard floors.
  3. Open what must be opened. Wet baseboard and sections of drywall come off so air can reach the cavity.
  4. Dry with controlled airflow. Air movers and dehumidifiers pull moisture out of materials. HEPA air scrubbers can filter dust stirred up by removal. See structural drying.
  5. Document everything. Readings, photos and a list of removed material go to you and, if you choose, to management or your adjuster.

Once readings are back to normal, repairs can begin. See water damage cleanup for how cleanup fits before rebuilding.

Insurance and paperwork in a shared building

Ask your agent or adjuster how your policy treats a sudden failure compared with a slow leak. If water came from another unit, ask whether your carrier or the other owner's carrier is involved.

Photograph the source, the ceiling, the floor and any belongings before cleanup. Write down the date you notified management. No one can promise coverage or approval.

What this means in Addison Circle

The Town of Addison describes Addison Circle as a walkable urban neighborhood of roughly 84 to 86 acres next to the Dallas North Tollway. What shapes the scope of a job

No two jobs match. These are the factors that decide how big the work is.

Number of units affected

Water source

Clean supply water is handled differently from drain water.

Materials involved

Mistakes to avoid

  • Waiting to tell management because the stain looks small.
  • Painting over a ceiling stain before it is checked for moisture.
  • Running only a household fan on wet drywall.
  • Discarding damaged items before photographing them.
  • Assuming the leak is fixed because the dripping stopped.
