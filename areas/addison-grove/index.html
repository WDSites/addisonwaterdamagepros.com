Water Damage Restoration in Addison Grove, Addison, TX

Multi-level townhomes and live/work units hide leaks behind finished walls and ceilings. Here is how water is found, removed and dried.

☎ (972) 945-2885

Addison Grove sits on Belt Line Road west of Midway Road, on the former Sam's Club site. It mixes owner-occupied townhomes, live/work units and an apartment community with ground-floor retail. Construction began around 2018, and the Town's 2022 housing FAQ noted more units still pending at that time.

Newer townhomes stack rooms on several levels, so a leak upstairs can end up in a kitchen or garage below. This page explains how that water travels and what a restoration team does about it. Call, say which floor the water is on, and ask about availability.

How water moves through a multi-level townhome

Water does not stay where it starts. A leak on the second floor follows framing, wiring holes and pipe chases down to the ceiling below. By the time a stain shows, the wall cavity and the floor system between levels may already be wet.

Shared walls add a second path. Water can cross to a neighboring unit through a party wall, which makes quick communication with that owner and your association important.

Bathrooms, laundry closets, air handlers and water heaters are common starting points. See water heater leak cleanup for what follows a tank failure.

A moisture meter and thermal camera confirm what is actually wet. These signs are a starting point.

Water and electrical safety in a stacked home

Ceilings that hold water can fail, and wet wiring can be live. Take these steps first.

  • Shut off the supply valve at the fixture or appliance, or the main shutoff for the unit, if you can reach it dry.
  • If you cannot stop the water, ask the Town of Addison to shut it off at the meter. Only an authorized agent should operate the meter.
  • Stay out of rooms where a ceiling is bulging or dripping near light fixtures. Turn off breakers only if the panel is dry and reachable.
  • Do not use a plugged-in appliance standing in water.
  • If gas is in the air, get out and call the gas utility or 911 from outside the home.
  • Call 911 if a ceiling is collapsing or anyone is hurt.

Extraction and drying in a finished interior

Finished homes need care because paint, trim and flooring are already in place. Industry practice follows ANSI/IICRC S500.

  1. Map the moisture. Moisture meters and a thermal camera trace water from the stain back to the source and into neighboring walls.
  2. Extract standing water. Extractors pull water from flooring, and small holes may be cut to release water trapped in a ceiling.
  3. Open only what is needed. Small drywall cuts or removed baseboards let air reach wet cavities. See drywall water damage for how those cuts are decided.
  4. Dry with air and dehumidifiers. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings come down, and HEPA air scrubbers can handle dust from any demolition.
  5. Record readings. Daily readings and photos support your claim file.

Drying is only half the job. The water damage repair page covers what comes after.

Live/work units and business areas

A live/work unit adds a space where you may keep equipment, inventory or client records. Move or cover valuables early and photograph them before they go anywhere. Ask your insurer whether your homeowner policy and any business policy treat that space differently.

If a ground-floor retail space or a shared commercial area is affected, building management may need to be involved. Water in a business setting is covered on the commercial water damage page.

HOA, owner and insurance documentation

Who pays can depend on where the failure happened. A shared wall, roof or common line may fall to the association, while a fixture inside your unit is usually yours. Read your governing documents and ask your association manager before work starts.

Ask your agent or adjuster how your policy treats a sudden burst compared with a slow leak. Take photos of the source, the stained ceiling and wet contents before cleanup. Keep failed parts when you can. No one on this page can promise coverage.

What this means in Addison Grove

Addison Grove is a Town and developer name, and the Town Council approved the project. The Town's housing FAQ from July 2022 counts 11,432 dwelling units in Addison, with 341 condo stacked flats and 342 townhomes among them, so multi-unit living is common across town.

The Town of Addison says plumbing work such as replacing water or sewer lines needs a permit, as does adding or removing walls. Cosmetic work and fixture swaps do not. No flood events were found for Addison Grove, and its flood zone status is not verified. The FEMA Flood Map Service Center lets you look up your own address, and standard homeowner policies generally exclude flood.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Painting over a ceiling stain before the source is found.
  • Ignoring a small leak at an air handler or condensate line. The AC leak water damage page shows what that can lead to.
  • Closing up a cut wall before it reads dry.
  • Starting work without telling the association.
