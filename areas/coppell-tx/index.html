Denton Creek and Coppell's flood history

The City of Coppell says four major tributaries cross the city: Denton Creek, Grapevine Creek, Cottonwood Creek and the Elm Fork of the Trinity River. In June 2015, heavy rain and runoff from Lake Grapevine pushed Denton Creek into hundreds of Coppell homes, according to CBS News and the Dallas Morning News.

If your home backs to a creek or greenbelt, look up your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center. The city also requires a floodplain development permit before any work in a floodplain, so ask Engineering before you rebuild near a creek.

A 2020 city release relayed by Patch said standard homeowners policies exclude rising water and that flood policies carry a 30-day waiting period. Buy flood coverage before the storm, not during it.

What February 2021 taught Coppell

The city reported about 750 water event calls from February 15 to 18, 2021, and roughly 1,200 water meters shut off. Coppell was not under a boil notice, and the city later announced a water and sewer credit and waiver for pipe-break usage.

For you, the lesson is practical: know where your main shutoff is, and after a pipe break ask the city utility whether any leak adjustment applies to your bill. The emergency water removal page covers what a crew does after a burst pipe.

Replacing a water heater in Coppell

The City of Coppell's water heater permit document says a permit is required, including for replacements, and that new and replacement units need an expansion tank and a drip pan piped to the outside. It also calls for a final inspection, and contractors must be registered with the city and state licensed.

That matters after a leak, because a failed heater often needs replacing the same week. Ask any plumber whether the permit is included in the quote.
