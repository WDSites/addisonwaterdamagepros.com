Water Damage Restoration in Les Lacs, Addison, TX

Rain that enters at ground level is a different job from a pipe leak. Here is what happens next and what the Town's reports actually say.

Les Lacs is a residential neighborhood of single-family homes, with a pond, a park and a set of streets the Town has studied for drainage. If heavy rain pushes water into your house, the first thing to know is that stormwater is handled differently from clean supply water.

Call and describe what you see, and ask about availability. While you wait, stay out of standing water, keep power off to wet areas and photograph the water line before anything moves.

Why stormwater is its own category

Water that comes in from outside has crossed yards, driveways and gutters. It can carry soil, fertilizer, oil and bacteria. Restoration teams treat it as contaminated, which changes what gets cleaned and what gets removed.

Porous material such as carpet pad, insulation and low drywall usually comes out. Hard surfaces get cleaned and dried. A backed-up floor drain is a separate problem, explained under sewage backup cleanup. The storm water damage page covers the rest.

Ground-level entry

Water that enters at doors, garage thresholds or low windows reaches flooring first. It then wicks up into baseboard and the bottom of walls. A moisture meter shows how high it climbed, which is often higher than the visible stain.

What the Town has documented near Les Lacs

The Town of Addison's 2017 Rawhide Creek report, prepared by Halff Associates, models street ponding in the Les Lacs neighborhood in a 100-year storm. It shows depths up to about 2 feet at Waterside Ct and 13 residences potentially flooded, partly from Les Lacs Pond spilling. This is a computer model and not a record of a flood. We found no dated flood event specific to Les Lacs.

The Town then built the Rawhide Creek Basin Drainage and ADA Improvements Project on Waterside Ct, Waterford Dr, Les Lacs Ave, Beau Park Ln and Brookwood Ln, with an underground detention structure at the end of the trail on Marsh Lane. Construction began in November 2022 and was described as substantially complete by December 2023.

Listing sites describe homes mostly from the 1980s and 1990s, along Proton Drive, plus a Les Lacs Garden Homes section. Les Lacs Park sits at Beltway Dr and Proton Dr. FEMA flood zone status is not verified. The FEMA Flood Map Service Center lets you look up your own address, and standard homeowner policies generally exclude flood, so ask your agent.

Storm safety steps for your home

Stormwater and electricity are a dangerous mix.

  • Stay out of standing water that may touch outlets, cords or appliances.
  • Turn off breakers only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water. Otherwise call an electrician or the utility.
  • If water is coming from a broken line, shut off the supply valve. For the meter, the Town of Addison handles it, because only an authorized agent may turn water on or off there.
  • Wear boots and gloves, because stormwater can carry bacteria.
  • A gas smell means everyone leaves first, and the call to the gas utility or 911 is made from outside.
  • Call 911 if anyone is in danger or the water is rising around you.

What a restoration team does after stormwater

The sequence is the same on most jobs, but the removal is heavier when water is contaminated. Industry practice follows ANSI/IICRC S500.

  1. Assess the water. The team notes where it came from and how long it sat, which sets the contamination level.
  2. Extract. Truck-mounted or portable extractors pull standing water from floors and carpet. See water removal for how this stage works.
  3. Remove porous material. Pad, soaked insulation and low drywall are cut out and bagged.
  4. Clean and treat surfaces. Hard surfaces and framing are cleaned before drying starts.
  5. Dry the structure. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until moisture meter readings match a dry reference. Details are on structural drying.
  6. Document. Photos, readings and a list of removed items are kept for your adjuster.

Modeled versus recorded, and why it matters to you

A model estimates what could happen in a rare storm. A record says what did happen. For Les Lacs, the Town report is the first kind. Do not read it as proof that your street has flooded, and do not read the drainage work as proof that it never will.

Know where your yard drains, keep gutters and downspouts clear, and look at your own address on FEMA's map tool. Large floods are covered on flood damage restoration.

Insurance after a storm

Standard homeowner policies generally exclude flood, which is water rising from outside. Wind-driven rain through a damaged roof or a burst pipe may be treated differently. Ask your agent or adjuster how your policy classifies your event.

Photograph the water line on walls, the floor and your belongings before cleanup. Keep samples of removed material if you can. Nobody can promise coverage or approval.

What shapes the scope

These factors decide how large a stormwater job becomes.

Water category

Outside water means more removal and cleaning.

Depth and duration

Deeper or longer water reaches higher into walls.

Flooring and pad

Carpet and pad usually go. Tile and slab may dry in place.

Wall construction

Insulation in walls adds work.

Mistakes and prevention

  • Walking through standing water to check the damage.
  • Keeping wet carpet pad in place because the top looks fine.
  • Running a household fan and calling it dry.
  • Ignoring a musty smell a few days later.
  • Skipping downspout and gutter checks before storm season.
