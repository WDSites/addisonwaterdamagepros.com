What to do when rain gets into the house

Water that comes in from outside after a storm is treated differently from a clean supply line leak. It can carry soil and debris, so a restoration team usually removes porous material that touched it rather than trying to save every piece.

Start by getting people and pets out of the wet area. Then photograph before you move or toss anything, with a few wide shots and a few close shots of the waterline. Wet padding holds water long after carpet feels dry, and water wicks up drywall above the visible line. See carpet water damage and drywall water damage for those materials.

Storm and electrical safety before you touch anything

Water and power do not mix, and a wet house can hide hazards. Go through these before cleanup.

  • Do not walk through standing water if outlets, cords or appliances are in it. Turn off breakers only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.
  • If water is still coming from a supply line, shut off the valve. If you cannot, ask the Town of Addison to shut off water at the meter. Only an authorized agent should turn the meter, so do not wrench it yourself.
  • Call an electrician before restoring power to anything that sat in water.
  • When there is a gas odor, leave the house and use your phone outside to reach the gas utility or 911.
  • Call 911 for downed lines, rising water around the home or anyone in danger.
  • Wear boots and gloves, and keep children away from outside water that came indoors.

How a restoration team removes and dries the water

The sequence below is how a restoration team normally works. Industry practice follows ANSI/IICRC S500, and your own job may differ.

  1. Survey the damage. Moisture meters and a thermal camera show which walls, floors and cabinet bases are wet, including areas you cannot see.
  2. Extract standing water. Truck mounted or portable extractors pull water off hard floors and out of carpet and padding.
  3. Remove what cannot be saved. Wet padding, soaked insulation and swollen board come out so they stop holding water.
  4. Dry the structure. Air movers push air across surfaces and into wall cavities while dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the air.
  5. Clean and treat surfaces. Remaining surfaces are cleaned, and HEPA air scrubbers may run to handle dust from demolition.
  6. Monitor and document. Readings are repeated until wet areas match a dry reference. See water removal for how the first stage works.

Once the structure is dry, rebuilding can begin. The water damage repair page describes that stage.

Documenting a storm loss for your insurer

Ask your agent or adjuster how your policy treats sudden water from a storm compared with slow seepage. Rain that enters through a roof opening may be handled differently from water that rises from the ground, so do not assume.

Photograph rooms before cleanup, note the date and time the water arrived, and keep a list of contents that were wet. Save damaged items until your adjuster says otherwise. A restoration team can supply readings and a removal log, but nobody on this page can promise coverage or approval.

Mold, Town permits and a nearby neighborhood

The Texas Department of Insurance says mold can start growing within a day after a leak, which is one reason wet material should not sit. Read more on the mold remediation page.

The Town of Addison requires permits for plumbing work such as replacing water or sewer lines, and for adding or removing walls. Midway Meadows shares Les Lacs Park with its neighbor, and the Les Lacs page covers storm drainage there.

Mistakes to avoid after a storm

  • Running a household vacuum on wet carpet or standing water.
  • Leaving wet padding in place because the carpet surface feels dry.
  • Throwing away ruined items before taking photos.
  • Painting or putting down new flooring before walls and subfloor read dry.
