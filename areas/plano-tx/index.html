Water damage restoration in Plano, TX follows the calendar. Winter freezes burst pipes, spring storms push creeks over their banks, and appliances fail all year. Addison Water Damage Pros dispatches to every Plano ZIP code, 75023, 75024, 75025, 75074, 75075, 75093 and 75094, on a 24/7 line.

Plano covers about 72 square miles of mostly Collin County, so the details below are organized by season.

Winter: Plano's freeze and thaw calendar

The National Weather Service reports an average first freeze of November 22 and an average last freeze of March 12 at DFW Airport, with about 29 freezes per season. Plano's own numbers vary a little, but the pattern holds across North Texas.

In February 2021 the DFW stretch included a low of -2 degrees on February 16 and two periods at or below freezing of 93 and 139 hours. Pipes in attics, exterior walls and garages are the usual failures, and many break when they thaw, not when they freeze.

Between November and March, insulate exposed lines and check for slow leaks after any hard freeze. If a pipe does burst, emergency water removal is the first call.

Spring: storms and creek overflow

The National Weather Service says severe weather peaks in spring across the Metroplex, with flooding occurring nearly every year. On May 29, 2015, Rowlett Creek overflowed after several inches of overnight rain and flooded Bob Woodruff Park and Oak Point Park, plus some backyards and streets, according to NBC DFW.

Plano's floodplain page keeps flood map information and elevation certificates and recommends flood insurance through the National Flood Insurance Program. Texas Department of Insurance guidance is that standard home policies do not cover floods, so check your policy in winter, not during the storm.

When storm water does enter, see flood damage restoration.

All year: water heater and appliance leaks

The City of Plano's water heater installation handout lists a drain pan when the heater sits above the first floor, and a temperature and pressure relief valve with a drain line. The handout does not state a permit rule, so ask Building Inspections before replacing a unit.

Those details matter because an attic or upstairs closet heater without a working pan turns a small drip into a ceiling stain below. A water mitigation crew finds the wet drywall and insulation that the stain does not show.

Washing machine hoses, dishwasher lines and refrigerator ice-maker lines cause the rest. Replacing rubber hoses with braided lines is cheap insurance.

If water is still spreading: before we arrive

These four steps help while a crew is on the way.

Shut it off

Close the fixture valve or the main. A ceiling leak means shutting water off to the floor above.

Mind the power

Turn off breakers for wet areas only from a dry spot, and stay out if water touches the panel.

Lift and record

Raise valuables above the wet line and photograph everything first.
