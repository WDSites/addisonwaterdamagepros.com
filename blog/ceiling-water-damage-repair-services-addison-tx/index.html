Ceiling Water Damage Repair Services in Addison, TX: What They Do and How to Compare Them

A repair service should confirm the ceiling is dry before any new drywall goes up. Here is the sequence, the scope questions, and the cost factors.

By the Addison Water Damage Pros team · Updated 2026

☎ (972) 945-2885

Ceiling water damage repair services in Addison, TX replace or restore the wet drywall, insulation and finish above you once the leak is stopped and the framing is dry. The work follows a fixed order: confirm dry, cut out, insulation, board, tape, mud and texture, then prime and paint.

Most disputes between homeowners and providers come from skipped steps or vague scopes. This guide shows what each step involves, how to read two competing scopes side by side, and which questions to ask before anyone opens a toolbox.

What a repair service does, in order

A good job is sequenced so each step protects the next one. If the order changes, ask why.

  1. Confirm the source is fixed and the ceiling is dry. The tech reads the drywall and the framing above it with a moisture meter, often backed by a thermal camera. Readings are logged. Painting over a ceiling that still reads wet is how stains and bubbles come back.
  2. Cut out what cannot be saved. Sagging, soft or crumbling board is cut back to the nearest joists, usually in a square or rectangle with straight edges. Cutting past the visible stain to dry edges gives the patch something solid to attach to.
  3. Check and replace insulation. Wet batts or blown insulation lose most of their value and hold moisture against the framing. Damp material is bagged and removed, then new insulation goes in at the original depth.
  4. Hang new board. Drywall is cut to fit, screwed to the joists, and set flush. Ceilings are often half-inch or five-eighths board, so matching the thickness matters for a flat finish.
  5. Tape, mud and sand. Paper or mesh tape covers the seams, then joint compound goes on in two or three thin coats. Each coat needs to dry and be sanded before the next, which is why a small patch can still take more than one day.
  6. Match the texture. Popcorn, orange peel, knockdown and skip trowel are all sprayed or hand applied. A good match is tested on scrap first, because texture that does not blend shows under side light.
  7. Prime and paint. A stain-blocking primer goes over the repair, then ceiling paint goes edge to edge, not just on the patch. Painting the full ceiling plane avoids a visible square.

Why confirming dry comes first

A repair crew and a drying crew do different jobs. If the ceiling cavity is still wet, the first call belongs to water mitigation, which uses air movers and dehumidifiers to pull moisture from the framing and insulation. Repair starts after that.

The Texas Department of Insurance notes that mold can begin growing within a day after a leak. A ceiling closed up over damp insulation gives it a dark, warm place to start. That is the practical reason a reputable provider reads moisture before it sells you new board.

How to compare scopes between providers

Two quotes for the same ceiling can differ by a lot and still describe different jobs. Line them up step by step. If one skips insulation, primer or texture, that is the gap, not a bargain.

A solid written scope names the room, the approximate area, the board thickness, the insulation type, the texture style and the paint scope. It also says whether the ceiling will be painted edge to edge and whether furniture moving and debris hauling are included.

Look for these lines in writing

Moisture readings and photos taken before work starts. A statement of who handles drying if the cavity is still wet. Who pulls any permit that applies. Who cleans up drywall dust, which travels farther than most people expect.

If a scope only says "repair ceiling," ask for a line-by-line version before you sign. Vague wording is where change orders live.

What drives the cost of ceiling repair

No honest provider can give a real number from a text message. These are the factors that move the total.

Size of the damaged areaA patch the size of a dinner plate and a room-wide replacement are priced as different jobs, and so is the height of the ceiling.
Water category and how long it satClean supply-line water is simpler than water that carried dirt or sewage through the ceiling, and long exposure adds removal.
Insulation and framing conditionWet insulation must come out. Damaged joists or sheathing add carpentry.
Texture and paint matchHand texturing takes skill and time, and a full-ceiling repaint adds labor and materials.
Access and drying timeVaulted ceilings, stairwells, second-story rooms and extra coats of compound all lengthen the schedule.

Sudden and accidental damage, such as a burst supply line above the ceiling, is often covered by home policies. Gradual leaks often are not. Check your policy or ask your insurer before permanent repairs, and photograph the damage first.

Questions to ask before you hire

These questions take five minutes and sort out most problems before they start.

  • Are you licensed and insured, and can you send proof of both?
  • Are you registered with the Town of Addison for this project, and who is the license holder responsible for the work?
  • Does this repair need a permit, and who will pull it?
  • Will you measure moisture before you close the ceiling, and will I get the readings and photos?
  • What happens if the framing or insulation turns out worse than expected?
  • Is the paint scope edge to edge, and how will you match the texture?

What this means in Addison

The Town of Addison requires contractors to register with the Town before starting a project, and the license holder stays responsible for the work. That gives you a concrete thing to ask for: the name of the registered contractor on your job, not just the name of a crew that shows up.

Permit rules matter too. Addison Development Services lists plumbing work, including replacing water or sewer lines, as needing a permit, while painting, wallpapering and other cosmetic work do not. Drywall replacement is not spelled out on the Town's list, so ask the Town's Building Inspection staff whether your ceiling repair needs one. If the leak came from a plumbing repair, that part may.
