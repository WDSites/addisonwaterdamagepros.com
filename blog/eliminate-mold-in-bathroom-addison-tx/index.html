How to Eliminate Mold in a Bathroom in Addison, TX

Fix the moisture, clean small surface spots the safe way, and know when the job belongs to a licensed professional.

By the Addison Water Damage Pros team · Updated 2026

☎ (972) 945-2885

To eliminate mold in a bathroom in Addison, TX, fix the moisture that feeds it first, then clean small surface patches with a household cleaner, and call a licensed professional if the growth is large or keeps returning. Mold needs moisture, so cleaning without a fix is a short-term cosmetic move.

This guide covers causes, safe cleaning, what not to do, the Texas size threshold, and a prevention checklist.

Find the cause before you pick up a sponge

Bathrooms make mold easy. Hot showers fill the room with vapor, tile and grout hold droplets, and a small exhaust fan may move less air than you think. Mold needs moisture, organic material to feed on and time. The Texas Department of Insurance says mold can start within a day after a leak.

Walk the room and look for the water. Is the fan weak or vented into the attic? Is caulk cracked at the tub? Is there a drip under the vanity? Condensation on a cold exterior wall or an AC vent can do the same job as a leak.

Common moisture sources to check

Check each one with a flashlight and a dry paper towel.

  • Exhaust fan: hold a single square of tissue to the grille while the fan runs. If it does not hold, the fan is weak or blocked. Fans that discharge into the attic instead of outdoors dump moisture there.
  • Caulk and grout: look for gaps, black lines or peeling at the tub, shower pan and window sill. Water sneaks behind them.
  • Supply lines and drains: touch the shutoff valves and traps under the sink for dampness.
  • Toilet base and wax seal: a soft floor or a stain near the base can mean a slow leak.
  • Condensation: cold surfaces, an uninsulated outer wall or a supply air register aimed at the shower can all bead water.

Safe cleaning of a small surface area

These steps are for visible growth on hard, non-porous surfaces such as tile, glass, a tub, or a sealed fixture.

  1. Ventilate. Open the window, run the fan, and keep the door to the rest of the house closed so spores do not spread.
  2. Protect yourself. Wear rubber gloves, safety glasses and an N95 mask. Long sleeves help.
  3. Choose one cleaner. Dish soap and water, a bathroom mold cleaner used as labeled, or white vinegar all work on hard surfaces. Never mix cleaners. Bleach with ammonia or with vinegar can release dangerous gases.
  4. Scrub and wipe. Use a stiff brush on grout and a damp cloth on flat surfaces. Work from clean to dirty areas.
  5. Dry everything. Wipe the surface and run the fan or a small fan for several hours. Moisture left behind restarts the problem.
  6. Bag the waste. Seal used rags and sponges in a plastic bag before they go in the trash.

The 25 square foot line in Texas

Texas licenses mold assessors and mold remediators through the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR). Remediation of 25 contiguous square feet or more of visible mold generally needs a licensed remediator, while areas smaller than that may be cleaned by unlicensed people, including a homeowner.

Sampling the air or visible growth counts as assessment, and one licensed company or person generally cannot do both assessment and remediation on the same project. That rule protects you, because the person who tests is not the person selling the cleanup.

What not to do

A few common moves make things worse.

  • Do not paint over mold. The paint peels and the growth continues underneath.
  • Do not mix bleach, ammonia, vinegar or other cleaners in one bucket.
  • Do not dry-scrub or sand moldy drywall. It sends spores through the house.
  • Do not keep cleaning porous materials like soft drywall, ceiling tile or carpet padding that stay stained. These usually have to be removed.
  • Do not skip the leak repair. A cleaned surface above a leaking pipe will grow again.
  • Do not use a fan to blow across active growth toward other rooms.

Questions about symptoms belong with a physician, not with a cleaning guide.

When to call a licensed professional

Call when the growth is 25 contiguous square feet or more, when it returns after cleaning, when it covers drywall, ceilings or the area behind a vanity, or when a leak soaked the wall. Remediation in practice follows ANSI/IICRC S520, with containment, negative air, HEPA filtration and removal of damaged porous material. You can ask any company whether its crews work to that standard.

A mold remediation team can also look for the moisture source, and a water damage repair crew can rebuild what comes out. Confirm each company's state license through TDLR's Verify a License search.

Prevention checklist

Habits matter more than products. No method guarantees mold stays away, but these steps cut the moisture it needs.

  • Run the exhaust fan during every shower and for 20 to 30 minutes afterward.
  • Squeegee shower walls and wipe the tub edge.
  • Re-caulk the tub, shower and sink joints when they crack or darken.
  • Hang towels to dry, not on the floor or a hook inside a small closet.
  • Fix drips under the sink and at the toilet base right away.
  • Leave the door open after showers when the room is humid.
  • Check that the fan vents outdoors, and consider a larger fan if the room fogs for a long time.
