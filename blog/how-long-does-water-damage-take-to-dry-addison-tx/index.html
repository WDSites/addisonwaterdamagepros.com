How Long Does Water Damage Take to Dry in Addison, TX?

A wet house is not dry when the surface feels dry. Here are realistic ranges by material and the readings that tell you when drying is finished.

By the Addison Water Damage Pros team · Updated 2026

☎ (972) 945-2885

Most water damage in Addison, TX takes about three to five days of active drying with air movers and dehumidifiers, though a small clean-water spill can finish sooner and a soaked floor or slab can run well past a week. Those numbers move with the material, the amount of water and the conditions in the house.

The better question is not how many days but what the meters say. This guide gives ranges by material, explains why dry to the touch misleads, and shows how a crew decides the job is done.

Drying ranges by material

These ranges assume the water source is stopped and equipment is running. Treat every number as a starting guess, since depth of saturation, room size and water category all change the result.

Why a surface that feels dry is not dry

Water evaporates from the surface first, so a floor or wall can feel normal while the material underneath still holds water. Drywall paper dries fast. The gypsum behind it and the lumber behind that do not.

That hidden moisture is the reason follow-up problems appear weeks later: warped boards, peeling paint, musty smells. TDI says mold can start growing within a day after a leak, which is why you want to confirm dryness with a meter and not your hand. The water mitigation process exists to close that gap between looks dry and is dry.

Moisture meters and drying goals

A pin meter reads moisture inside wood and drywall. A pinless meter scans a wider patch without making holes. A thermal camera shows cool, wet areas as color, which helps find water but does not give a moisture number on its own.

ANSI/IICRC S500 (the published standard for water damage restoration, Fifth Edition 2021) frames drying around a goal. The crew takes readings from an unaffected area of the same material, then dries the wet area until it reaches that dry standard. Ask any company whether its crews work to S500 and whether they log readings each day.

Daily readings also let the crew pull equipment as areas finish. Equipment that keeps running after a room reaches its goal only adds to the bill.

What each piece of equipment does and where it goes

Placement matters as much as the number of machines.

Air movers

High-velocity fans set low along wet walls and across wet floors, angled to push air along the surface so water leaves the material.

LGR dehumidifiers

These pull the evaporated water out of the air. They work best in a closed-off drying zone with doors shut and no outside air coming in.

Wall cavity drying

Small holes or lifted baseboards let dry air into the cavity behind the drywall, where framing and insulation stay wet longest.

Floor drying mats

Mats pull water up through wood flooring or the pad on a slab, used where hardwood might be saved.

Why humidity changes everything in summer

Drying is a trade of water from material to air. Warm air holds more moisture than cool air, so a hot day can load a room fast, but only if the air already has room to take more. On a muggy day the outside air can be nearly as wet as the room you are trying to dry.

That is why a crew keeps doors and windows closed, runs dehumidifiers and keeps the air conditioner on during a summer job. Opening windows to help it dry often slows things down. If your AC is off because water reached the air handler, tell the crew so they can plan around it.

What you can do while drying runs

Small actions protect the schedule:

  • Keep the equipment plugged in and the doors of the drying zone closed.
  • Do not move air movers or turn them off to quiet the house.
  • Keep the thermostat where the crew set it.
  • Hold off on new flooring, paint or trim until the readings are signed off.
  • Report any new smell, stain or soft spot on the same day.

What drives the length and cost of drying

Cost follows the same factors as time: water category (clean, gray or contaminated), the area affected, which materials are involved, the number of drying days and pieces of equipment, and how much rebuild follows. NWS Fort Worth lists the freeze-free period as running from about March to November, and severe weather peaks in spring. That means most Addison water losses happen while the AC is running and windows are shut, which is the right setup for dehumidifier drying. Keep the AC on.
