Mold Mildew Removal in Addison, TX: What Cleans and What Goes

A surface-by-surface guide to cleaning versus replacing, with humidity control basics and what to do when a lender or buyer asks for paperwork.

By the Addison Water Damage Pros team · Updated 2026

☎ (972) 945-2885

Mold mildew removal in Addison, TX comes down to one question per surface: can it be cleaned, or does it have to come out? Hard, sealed surfaces usually clean. Porous materials that hold growth usually get replaced.

Homeowners often use mildew for a flat, powdery surface growth and mold for something deeper or fuzzier. Both need moisture. This post covers surfaces, humidity, and when a cleanup becomes a remediation job.

Mold versus mildew in plain terms

People commonly call light gray or white powdery growth on shower tile or a window sill mildew, and thicker, darker or fuzzy growth mold. It is a homeowner distinction, not a lab one, and you cannot reliably tell them apart by looking. Both need moisture and something to grow on.

This post makes no health claims. Questions about symptoms belong with a physician. The practical rule is the same for both: remove the moisture, clean what can be cleaned, and take out what cannot.

A basic cleanup for hard surfaces

For small areas on tile, glass, sealed fixtures or painted wood trim.

  1. Fix the moisture first. A leak, damp cabinet or weak fan will bring growth back within weeks.
  2. Set up the room. Open a window, run a fan to the outside, and wear gloves, safety glasses and an N95.
  3. Use one cleaner. Dish soap and water, white vinegar or a labeled mold cleaner. Never mix chemicals, especially bleach with ammonia or acids.
  4. Scrub and rinse. Use a brush or cloth, then wipe with clean water.
  5. Dry completely. A dehumidifier or fan for several hours finishes the job. Damp leftovers restart the cycle.

Humidity control basics

Indoor relative humidity above roughly 60 percent for long periods favors growth, and many guides suggest staying between 30 and 50 percent. A cheap hygrometer from a hardware store tells you where your house sits.

In summer, an AC system pulls moisture from the air, but an oversized unit can short cycle and leave a room clammy. A clogged condensate line or a pan overflow can wet a ceiling or closet. Running bath and kitchen fans, fixing dryer vents and keeping furniture a few inches off exterior walls all help.

Signs a cleanup is turning into a remediation job

Look for these.

  • Growth covers 25 contiguous square feet or more, the Texas line where a licensed remediator is generally needed.
  • The materials are soft, stained through, crumbling or swollen.
  • Growth returns within a few weeks after cleaning.
  • A musty smell persists with no visible source, which can mean a wet cavity.
  • A recent leak soaked drywall, carpet or insulation.
  • The growth is inside the HVAC system or ducts.

At that point, mold remediation crews set containment, filter the air with HEPA equipment, remove porous material and clean the rest. If the cause was a leak or flood, water damage cleanup covers the wet material and drying.

Lender and home-sale situations

Buyers, appraisers and lenders sometimes ask for mold to be addressed before a closing. Ask your lender or agent exactly what documentation is needed, because requirements vary by loan type and contract.

In Texas, a licensed remediator issues a Certificate of Mold Damage Remediation after qualifying work, and licensed assessors and remediators must provide a Consumer Mold Information Sheet before starting. One licensed company or person generally cannot do both assessment and remediation on the same project, so a lender asking for testing and removal will typically need two companies.
