Water Damage Insurance in Addison, TX: What to Know Before You Call

Coverage depends on how the water got in, how fast you acted and what you documented. Here is what Texas regulators tell homeowners.

By the Addison Water Damage Pros team · Updated 2026

☎ (972) 945-2885

Water damage insurance in Addison, TX is the part of a homeowner or renter policy that responds to water losses, and how it applies depends on the cause. The Texas Department of Insurance says most policies cover sudden and accidental damage, such as a burst pipe or toilet overflow, and usually not gradual leaks.

No article can say whether your claim will be approved, and neither can a restoration company. This guide lays out the questions to ask, the records to keep and the order of steps that protects your file.

Sudden and accidental versus gradual

A sudden and accidental loss happens at a definable moment: a supply line snaps, a washer hose bursts, a toilet overflows. A gradual loss builds over time, such as a pinhole leak in a wall or a slow drip under a sink that nobody noticed for months.

Insurers draw this line because gradual damage is often seen as a maintenance problem. If you are unsure which one you have, do not guess in your first call. Describe what you saw, when you saw it, and what you did, and let the adjuster decide. Our water mitigation crews document moisture patterns that show how far the water spread, which can help you describe the loss.

The flood exclusion and mold coverage quirks

TDI says home policies do not cover floods, so a separate flood policy is needed. If water came from rising or surface water, ask about flood coverage before assuming anything.

Mold has its own quirks. According to TDI, most home policies do not include mold cleanup and testing after a damaged item is removed, and insurers may let you add that coverage, so ask your agent. Mold that develops on a covered damaged item would be covered. The practical result is that the wording around mold deserves a close read well before a loss.

Your duty to protect the property

Policies expect you to limit further damage after a loss. A TDI blog notes that an insurer may deny a claim if you do not protect your property. TDI recommends turning the water off at the main and moving expensive items off the floor.

TDI also lists steps that fit a crew's first visit: stop the source quickly, remove standing water, dry the area promptly, move wet items to a dry area and pull up wet carpets and rugs. Mold can start growing within a day after a leak, so delay costs more than just time. Our water damage cleanup work follows that order.

Documentation checklist

Gather these before cleanup changes the scene:

  • Photos and video of the source, the water lines and every affected room, taken before anything is moved.
  • A list of damaged belongings with brand, age and where you bought them.
  • Moisture readings and a written scope from the restoration company.
  • Damaged items themselves, kept until the adjuster has seen them.
  • Receipts for emergency repairs, equipment, hotel stays and supplies.
  • A log of every call or email with your insurer, with dates and names of who you spoke with.

When to report, and temporary versus permanent repairs

Report hidden water damage quickly. TDI says you usually must report it within days after you first see it, so do not wait until the repair is finished. Call your insurer or agent as soon as the water is stopped, then follow their instructions.

TDI says to make temporary repairs to prevent further damage, such as tarping a roof or shutting off a line, but not permanent ones until the insurer inspects. Keep receipts for temporary work. Replacing drywall, flooring and cabinets should wait for the adjuster's visit, unless the insurer says otherwise in writing.

Mold claims and the certificate

If a claim involves mold, TDI says to confirm the mold professional has a state license, expect an inspection showing the mold was removed and the cause was fixed, and get a certificate of removal. Keep that certificate with your claim file, since lenders and buyers often ask for it.
