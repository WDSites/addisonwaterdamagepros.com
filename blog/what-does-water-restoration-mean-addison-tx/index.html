What Does Water Restoration Mean? Phases, Terms and Water Categories in Addison, TX

Companies use five terms for water damage work, and they are not interchangeable. Here is what each one covers and the order they happen in.

By the Addison Water Damage Pros team · Updated 2026

☎ (972) 945-2885

Water restoration in Addison, TX means returning a water-damaged home to a dry, clean, safe and livable condition. It is a sequence of phases, not one job: stop the damage, remove the water, dry the structure, clean what is salvageable, then repair or rebuild.

Quotes and phone calls get confusing because providers use different words for different slices of that sequence. Knowing the terms helps you compare offers and ask the right questions before anyone starts work.

The phases at a glance

Phases overlap in real jobs, but the order stays the same. Skipping ahead is the usual source of repeat problems.

Restoration (the whole)Return the home to pre-loss conditionAll of the above, coordinatedProject lead, adjuster if a claim is open, homeowner

Some companies do all phases. Others do only mitigation or only rebuild.

Mitigation: stop it from getting worse

Mitigation is the emergency phase. A crew finds the extent of the water with moisture meters and a thermal camera, extracts standing water, and sets drying equipment. The aim is to limit what is lost, which is why it should start as soon as safely possible. Our water mitigation page covers the equipment and drying logs in more depth.

Mitigation can also include tearing out materials that cannot be dried in place, such as saturated padding or wet insulation. This is not demolition for its own sake. It lets air reach the framing so the structure can reach a dry standard.

Cleanup, repair and reconstruction

Cleanup deals with what the water touched. That can mean sanitizing surfaces, cleaning salvageable belongings, and controlling odor. If the water was dirty, cleanup is also a safety step, not just an appearance one.

Repair and reconstruction are the build-back. Repair usually means patching drywall, replacing insulation, laying flooring and painting. Reconstruction is bigger: moving walls, rebuilding cabinets, replacing subfloor. Our water damage repair team handles the interior side once drying is verified.

Why the order matters

Rebuilding over wet framing traps moisture inside new materials. That is how a repaired wall ends up with a musty smell or a new stain months later. A dry reading, logged and shown to you, is the gate between mitigation and repair.

The published standard behind the work

The industry reference is ANSI/IICRC S500, the standard for professional water damage restoration (Fifth Edition, 2021). It covers water categories, classes of loss, drying principles and documentation. It is a published standard, not a license.

Ask any company whether its crews work to S500. A good answer describes how they classify the water, set drying goals and record readings. A vague answer is information too. We do not claim a certification on anyone's behalf here, only that S500 is the document worth asking about.

Water categories in plain words

The category tells you how contaminated the water is, and that decides what can be saved.

Category 1: clean source

Water from a broken supply line, an overflowing sink with clean water, or a tub. It starts sanitary but does not stay that way for long.

Category 2: significant contamination

Water from a washing machine or dishwasher discharge, or clean water that has sat and picked up bacteria. Porous materials like carpet padding are often removed.

Category 3: grossly contaminated

Sewage, floodwater from outside, or standing water that has gone far past clean. Porous materials are generally removed, and people and pets should stay out.

Classes of loss and why the category can change

Category describes the water, and class describes how much and how fast it will dry. A small leak into one wall and a whole-floor soak need different equipment counts and different timelines.

Category can worsen over time. A clean supply-line break is Category 1 at first. If it runs through a ceiling, sits in insulation or goes unaddressed for a couple of days, it can move to a higher category. That is another reason to start early. TDI also notes that mold can start growing within a day after a leak.

Questions that show what a company actually does

Use these to see which phases you are being offered.

  • Do you do mitigation only, or does your quote include repair and rebuild?
  • Will you document moisture readings and photos at the start and each day after?
  • Do your crews work to ANSI/IICRC S500?
  • How do you classify the water category and the class of loss?
  • Who coordinates with my insurer if I open a claim, and what do I still need to send?
  • Are you licensed and insured, and who pulls any permits?

What this means in Addison

The Town of Addison water quality report says the Town purchases its water entirely from the City of Dallas. For a supply-line break, that means the water starts as treated drinking water and is typically Category 1. It still needs fast removal, because clean water that sits in insulation or padding moves up the categories.

On the rebuild side, the Town adopted the 2024 International Residential Code and 2024 International Plumbing Code with amendments for permits submitted on or after June 1, 2024. Contractors must register with the Town before starting a project. Ask who handles that, and whether your repair scope needs a permit.
