Carpet Water Damage Restoration in Addison, TX

Wet carpet is often salvageable when the water is clean and the drying starts fast. We extract, lift, dry the layers underneath, and tell you straight when replacement makes more sense.

Carpet water damage means water has soaked the carpet, the cushion under it, and often the subfloor below. Our 24/7 line is open, so you can call at any hour and we will dispatch a crew, with same-day response when available.

While you wait, keep off the soaked area, lift small furniture off the carpet if you can do it safely, and open closet doors so air can move. Do not run a household vacuum on wet carpet, and stay out of the room if you see water near outlets or power strips.

What water does to carpet, cushion and subfloor

Carpet is three layers working together: fiber, backing, and a cushion that acts like a sponge. Water passes through the fiber in seconds and settles in the cushion, where it stays wet long after the surface feels dry.

The subfloor, tack strips and baseboards hold moisture too. A carpet can look fine on top and still be wet at the edges, so we check every layer instead of trusting a touch test.

How it happens in North Texas homes

Common sources include a washer hose that lets go, a leaking water heater, a roof leak running down a wall, or storm water pushed under a door. The National Weather Service in Fort Worth notes that flooding occurs nearly every year somewhere in the Dallas/Fort Worth area.

First-hour safety steps for wet carpet

Before you worry about the carpet, deal with the hazards.

  • Shut off the water at the fixture valve or the main if the source is still running.
  • Switch off power to the wet room at the breaker if you can reach the panel without standing in water.
  • Do not use a plug-in vacuum, fan or space heater on wet carpet.
  • Call 911 for sparking, a burning smell or anyone shocked, and your gas utility for a gas odor.
  • If the main valve is stuck, call the Town of Addison for an emergency water turn-off, since only an authorized agent may work the meter.
  • Take photos before anything moves.

Why water category and time decide whether carpet is saved

Clean water from a supply line can often be dried in place if we reach it quickly. Gray water from a washer or dishwasher is a closer call. Black water from a sewage backup or outside flooding means the cushion goes, and the carpet usually goes too, because fiber and backing cannot be made safe again.

Time works against you in every case, and the Texas Department of Insurance notes that mold can start growing within a day after a leak. Cut pile, berber and synthetic backings react differently, so we judge each room on its readings. If you suspect growth, see mold remediation.

Our process for wet carpet

Industry practice under ANSI/IICRC S500 guides how we sequence the work. Here is what happens in your home.

  1. Find the water. We map wet areas with moisture meters and a thermal camera, then check the subfloor edges and the room next door.
  2. Extract. A weighted extraction wand and truck-mount or portable extractors pull water out of the fiber and cushion. See our water extraction service for the standing water side of the job.
  3. Lift and float. We pull the carpet back from the tack strip and float it over a clean air gap so the cushion and subfloor can be reached.
  4. Remove what cannot dry. Soaked cushion is usually cut out and bagged. Contaminated carpet is removed along with it.
  5. Dry with airflow and dehumidification. Air movers push air under the lifted carpet and LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture from the room. Our structural drying page covers how readings guide the equipment.
  6. Treat and clean. We apply antimicrobial treatment where it fits, clean the carpet that stays, and use HEPA air scrubbers if dust or odor is an issue.
  7. Re-lay or replace. Dry carpet is stretched back onto the tack strip. If it will not hold its stretch, we tell you so.

What changes the cost of carpet water damage

Insurance and carpet claims

Policies differ, so ask your agent or adjuster how yours treats the cause of the loss. Insurers often separate sudden events, such as a burst hose, from gradual leaks. Plumbing failures and flood are also treated differently, and standard homeowner policies generally do not cover flood.

Take wide and close photos of the wet carpet, the source and damaged belongings before we start. We record moisture readings and removed materials so your adjuster can see what was done.

Mistakes to avoid with wet carpet

  • Waiting a few days to see if it dries on its own.
  • Pointing a single box fan at the surface and calling it done.
  • Walking the soaked area and pushing water deeper into the cushion.
  • Steam cleaning over a wet pad, which adds more water.
