Emergency Water Removal in Addison, TX

Standing water pumped and extracted right now, before it reaches the subfloor and the walls.

Emergency water removal in Addison, TX means pumping and extracting standing water from a home as fast as the situation allows. Our 24/7 line is open now, and a dispatcher sends licensed and insured crews, with same-day response when available.

While you wait, stop the source, shut off the main water valve, lift valuables off the floor, and keep people and pets out of any water that may be contaminated. Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel while standing on dry ground.

When standing water is an emergency

Any water that is still moving, still rising or sitting on carpet is an emergency. Water wicks up drywall and baseboards, soaks into the pad under carpet, and finds its way into the subfloor. Every hour of delay raises the amount of material that has to be torn out later.

What to do while you wait for the crew

These steps are safe for most homeowners and they limit the damage before extraction starts.

  • Stop the source if you can reach it, or close the main shutoff.
  • Move rugs, electronics, books and small furniture to a dry room.
  • Put foil or plastic under the legs of furniture that has to stay on wet carpet.
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks so trapped water can escape.

How we pump and extract the water

The equipment depends on depth. Deeper water, such as several inches in a finished room, goes out first with submersible pumps and hoses run to a drain or the yard. Once the water is shallow, truck-mounted or portable extraction units pull the rest off hard floors and out of carpet.

On carpet we use weighted extractors, wand tools a technician presses down to squeeze water from the pad and backing. Wet vacs handle corners, stairs and tight closets. Drying equipment still has to finish the job, so ask about water mitigation once the pumps shut off.

Burst pipe water damage

Shut the main first. The Town of Addison says no one but an authorized agent may turn your water on or off at the water meter, so use the shutoff inside your home. It is typically in a garage, laundry room or closet, and a yard valve may sit under a green plastic cover in a flower bed.

A broken supply line can put out several gallons a minute, enough to cover a kitchen floor in minutes. Photograph the break, the water line on walls and every damaged item before cleanup starts.

A pipe under a sink or inside a wall needs more than extraction. Water runs down into the wall cavity and cabinet base where you cannot see it. Afterward a technician maps moisture with a meter and thermal camera, so wet drywall and insulation are found before mold starts. The professional who repairs the pipe should turn the water back on.

Water in a crawl space or the whole house

Standing water under a house is harder to judge. A crawl space may hold water from a plumbing leak, an irrigation line, or groundwater pushed in by a heavy storm. We pump it out, but the source still has to be found and fixed, or the water returns.

If the whole house is flooded, treat the water as contaminated until a technician says otherwise. Backflow from a drain or sewer line is a common cause. Do not walk through it, and read flood damage restoration for how those jobs are handled.

Town of Addison emergency water facts

The Town runs its own emergency line for the water system, which helps you decide who to call first.

Town crewsFor water main breaks, meter leaks and fire hydrant leaks, the Town says crews are available 24 hours and there is no charge. Report a leak at the street or meter to the Town first.
Freezing weatherThe Town will shut off your water on an emergency basis for a burst pipe, and it recommends turning sprinklers off when a freeze is forecast.

Safety before anyone steps into the water

Water and electricity do not mix, and sewage is a separate risk.

  • If water touches an electrical panel, outlets or a plugged-in appliance, stay out of the room.
  • Cut power only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water.
  • If you smell gas or see sparking, leave and call 911 or the utility before you call us.
  • If sewage is involved, keep children and pets out and do not touch the water.
  • Do not use a household vacuum on standing water.
