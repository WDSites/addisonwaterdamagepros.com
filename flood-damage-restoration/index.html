Flood damage restoration in Addison, TX is the removal of flood water, the tear-out of what it contaminated, and the cleaning and drying of everything that can be saved. We answer 24/7, and same-day response is available when crews are free.

While you wait, stay out of the water, keep children and pets away, and shut off the main water valve if a pipe is involved. Cut power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and you can reach it standing on dry ground.

When floodwater is an emergency

Floodwater is an emergency from the first inch. It can carry soil, runoff and sewage, and it soaks into drywall, insulation and subfloor quickly. Garages, ground-floor units and lower levels take the water first, and they are the hardest to dry once it has been sitting.

The National Weather Service says flooding occurs nearly every year across the Dallas/Fort Worth Metroplex, with severe weather peaking in spring. Drying has to start the same day, so keep the dispatch line handy before the next storm season arrives.

Basement and lower-level flooding cleanup

Many North Texas homes are built slab-on-grade, so a flooded lower level often means a garage, a ground-floor room or a unit at street level. If your home has a basement, below-grade room or walkout, water collects there first and may need a pump before anything else can start.

Do not walk into a flooded basement with the power on. Stairs can hide deep water, and outlets or appliances below the waterline can energize it. Call the utility or 911 if you suspect an electrical hazard.

Once the water is out, crews remove wet carpet and pad, cut drywall above the water line, and pull baseboards so walls can dry from the inside. Concrete floors are cleaned and dried but not torn out. Extraction is covered in more detail on our emergency water removal page. Wet insulation and trapped water in wall cavities are the usual hidden problems, so crews check with a moisture meter before closing anything up.

Sewage backup cleanup

A sewage backup is Category 3 water. Stay out of it, do not touch it, and keep people and pets away. Do not run taps or flush toilets, because that adds more water to the problem.

First find out whether the clog is in your own line or in the main. The Town of Addison says its crews respond to clogged or overflowing wastewater mains and there is no charge, so call the Town if you think the main is the cause. For a clog in your own plumbing, call a plumber to clear it before cleanup starts.

Cleanup is a removal job first. Porous materials the sewage touched, such as carpet, pad, drywall and insulation, are cut out and bagged. Hard surfaces like tile, concrete and sealed wood are scrubbed, disinfected and dried. Crews work in protective gear, and you should not start this work yourself.

What the equipment does

Pumps and extraction units remove standing water. Air movers push air across wet surfaces, and LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the air so the walls can release theirs. Moisture meters and thermal imaging find water behind finished surfaces, so crews know what is still wet before the equipment comes out. Equipment stays until readings show the structure is dry. Drying methods are explained on the water damage cleanup page.

Flood insurance and what to ask first

Home policies do not cover floods, according to the Texas Department of Insurance. A separate flood policy is needed. If you are unsure what you have, read your policy and ask your insurer or agent before cleanup, and keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them.

Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address to see the published flood map. That tells you whether flood coverage is worth pricing. Photograph everything before repairs, make temporary repairs only, and keep every receipt.
