Before the crew arrives

What to do while water is still spreading

Stop the source

Close the fixture shutoff or the main valve. The Town of Addison says the inside universal shutoff is typically in a garage, laundry room or closet, and some yards have a valve under a green plastic cover.

Think about power

Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel without standing in water. If water touches outlets or the panel, stay out and call 911 or the utility first.

Protect what you can

Lift valuables, rugs and electronics above the wet line. Photograph everything before you move it, because the photos help with an insurance claim.

Keep people out

Keep children and pets away from sewage or storm water. Contaminated water needs gloves, boots and a crew, not a mop.

Insurance claims

Documentation your insurer can read

Records that support a claim

We record moisture readings, take photos before and after tear-out, and write a scope of work. Texas Department of Insurance guidance is to photograph or video damage before repairs and to keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them.

Most homeowner and renter policies cover sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe or toilet overflow. Policies usually do not cover gradual leaks, and home policies do not cover floods, so check your policy and ask your insurer what applies to your loss.

For a large loss, such as water through several rooms or a vacant unit, the documentation matters even more. Ask your insurer about timing, because TDI notes that hidden water damage usually must be reported within days after you first see it.

Local to Addison

Water damage in a compact Dallas County town

Addison covers about 4.4 square miles of Dallas County, and Dallas borders it on the north, east and south. Dispatch routes cover Carrollton to the west, Richardson to the east, Plano to the north and Coppell to the west of Carrollton.

The Town of Addison buys its water entirely from the City of Dallas, according to the Town's water quality report. For you, that means a main break or a pressure problem may start on a utility line rather than inside your home, so report leaks at the meter to the Town and call us for the damage inside.

Town crews respond around the clock to water main breaks, meter leaks and clogged or overflowing wastewater mains, and the Town says there is no charge for those emergency services. Only an authorized agent may turn water on or off at the meter, so use your own shutoff valve first.

Repairs that touch plumbing may need a permit. The Town's building safety pages list water heater replacement and plumbing work as permit items, while cosmetic work such as painting and carpeting is not. Ask Building Inspection before you start, and expect a registered contractor.

Crews work around Addison Circle, Vitruvian Park and Les Lacs Park, along Belt Line Road and Midway Road, and near Addison Airport and the Dallas North Tollway corridor. A tight street grid helps, but it also means upstairs leaks in multi-unit buildings often reach neighbors fast. Call before the water travels.

North Texas also has a freeze history worth planning for. The National Weather Service recorded a low of -2 degrees at DFW Airport on February 16, 2021, with two stretches at or below freezing of 93 and 139 hours. Pipes that survive one freeze can fail when they thaw, so check for slow leaks after a hard freeze.
