Kitchen Water Damage Restoration in Addison, TX

We pull toe kicks, dry cabinet bases and subfloor, and remove what cannot be saved, so your kitchen can be rebuilt on a dry structure.

Kitchen water damage restoration in Addison covers the cabinets, floor and subfloor that water reaches when a sink, line or appliance fails. Our 24/7 line is open, including nights, weekends and holidays, because a kitchen holds water in places you cannot see.

While you wait, shut off the valve under the sink if you can reach it without standing in water. Keep power off to wet outlets and appliances, and move food and small items out of the wet zone.

Why a kitchen takes water differently than other rooms

A kitchen is built from boxes. Cabinets sit on a recessed base, appliances slide into alcoves, and the floor often runs under all of it. Water that starts at the sink or dishwasher moves down and out into spaces that never see air.

That is why a kitchen can look fine on the surface while the bases are wet. The visible puddle is small. The wet material behind the toe kick is not.

Toe kicks and cabinet bases

The toe kick is the low gap at the bottom of a cabinet run. Water collects there and soaks the bottom edge of each box. If your cabinets have particleboard boxes, they can swell and lose strength. Plywood boxes tolerate water better, but they still need to dry.

Flooring under the cabinets

Vinyl, laminate, tile and hardwood react differently. Water can run under a floor covering and sit on the subfloor for days. Laminate and wood may cup or lift, while tile may look untouched even when the layer below is wet.

First-hour kitchen safety steps

Kitchens combine water, outlets and heavy appliances. Take a minute before you start cleaning.

  • Turn off the supply valve under the sink or behind the appliance, if you can reach it dry.
  • If you cannot find or turn a valve, ask the Town of Addison to shut off water at the meter, and do not force the meter yourself.
  • Switch off breakers for the kitchen if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely. Otherwise call an electrician.
  • Do not touch a plugged-in appliance standing in water.
  • If you smell gas near a range, leave and call the gas utility or 911 from outside.
  • Photograph the cabinets, floor and appliances before moving anything.

How we dry and restore a wet kitchen

The goal is to dry what can be saved and remove what cannot, in that order. Industry practice under ANSI/IICRC S500 guides how we set drying goals.

  1. Find the wet area. Moisture meters and a thermal camera map cabinets, walls and floor, including areas beside and below the sink.
  2. Extract standing water. Portable extractors pull water from the floor and from open cabinet bases.
  3. Open the hidden spaces. We pull toe kicks, then drill or vent cabinet bases so air can move through the boxes.
  4. Protect what is worth protecting. Stone and solid countertops are covered and kept out of the way. Appliances are moved with care.
  5. Dry with mats and ports. Air movers, drying mats and cavity ports push warm air through bases and under flooring, while LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture from the room. The structural drying page explains this phase in more depth, see structural drying.
  6. Remove what will not dry. Swollen cabinet boxes, wet insulation and flooring that cannot dry come out so they do not hold moisture.
  7. Document the work. We record readings, photos and removed items for your adjuster.

Once the structure reads dry, a repair contractor can rebuild. See water damage repair for that stage.

Where the water came from matters

A sink supply line, a drain connection and a cracked pipe inside a wall are different problems. A plumber fixes the pipe or fitting, and we dry what it soaked. If an appliance caused the leak, the appliance leak page explains how the failure type changes cleanup.

Water category also matters. Clean supply water is treated differently from a dishwasher drain or a sink backup. Contaminated water means more removal of porous material, including cabinet bases and insulation.

What changes the cost

Kitchen jobs vary a lot. These are the factors that set the scope.

Cabinet material

Particleboard boxes may need replacing, while plywood boxes can often dry.

Flooring type and layers

Water category

Contaminated water means more removal and cleaning.

Time before drying began

Longer wet time means deeper saturation in boards and framing.

Spread to nearby rooms

Water that crossed into a dining room or hall adds area to dry.

Access to wet spaces

Islands, built-ins and heavy appliances take more labor to reach.

Kitchen water damage and insurance

Policies differ, so ask your agent or adjuster how yours treats a sudden pipe failure compared with a slow leak. Sudden events are often handled differently from long-term seepage.

Take photos of the leak source, the cabinets and the floor before cleanup. Keep damaged parts if you can. We add readings and a removal log. We do not promise coverage or approval.

What this means in Addison

The Town of Addison dispatches an operator for an emergency water shut-off at no charge, and Town crews are available around the clock. Only an authorized agent may turn water on or off at the meter, so call the Town if your inside valve is stuck.

Addison Development Services says plumbing work such as replacing water or sewer lines needs a permit, while cosmetic work such as cabinets, trim, carpeting and painting does not. Contractors must register with the Town before starting.

Replacing drywall or flooring is not spelled out on the Town's list, so ask Building Inspection. Permits run through the Town's Citizen Self Service portal.

Source type: Town of Addison utilities page and Development Services (Building Safety)

Mistakes to avoid

  • Mopping the visible water and leaving the toe kick closed.
  • Replacing a countertop or flooring before the cabinet bases are dry.
  • Using a household fan alone on wet cabinets.
  • Painting or sealing a damp cabinet.
  • Throwing away parts before photographing them.
