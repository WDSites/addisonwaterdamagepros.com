Mold Remediation in Addison, TX

Find the moisture, contain the mold, remove what cannot be cleaned, and document the work for your insurer or lender.

☎ (972) 945-2885

Mold remediation in Addison, TX is the controlled removal and cleaning of mold growth, paired with fixing the moisture that fed it. Crews contain the area, filter the air, take out materials that cannot be cleaned, and clean the surfaces that can.

Most mold calls start with a leak from a toilet, sink, AC condensate line, roof or siding. Our 24/7 line takes those calls, and a dispatcher can explain the next step in plain terms.

I see dark spots on my bathroom ceiling. Is it mold?

Dark or fuzzy spots on a bathroom ceiling, a closet wall or a baseboard often are mold, but looks alone cannot confirm it. Dirt, soot and old water stains can look the same. A musty smell, paint that bubbles, or a stain that returns after cleaning are common signs that moisture is still there.

The useful question is where the water is coming from. When a person asks how to get rid of the spots, the trade answer is mold remediation under ANSI/IICRC S520, the published standard for it. It means finding and fixing the moisture, containing the area, and removing the growth by method, not by wiping it with a spray.

Mold removal and black mold cleanup

Black color does not identify a species. Mold comes in many colors, and only lab work can name a species. We make no health claims about any type, and questions about symptoms belong to your physician. What we can do is remove the growth safely.

The work starts with containment. Crews hang plastic sheeting, tape seams, and run negative air with HEPA air filtration so spores are pulled out of the room instead of drifting to the rest of the house. Workers wear respirators and disposable suits, and the room is kept under negative pressure until the work is done.

Porous materials with growth, such as drywall, insulation, ceiling tile and carpet, are cut out and bagged, because mold grows into them. Non-porous surfaces like metal, glass, tile and sealed wood are cleaned with HEPA vacuuming and damp wiping. Afterward the area is dried and checked with a moisture meter before containment comes down.

Mold after water damage

Mold needs moisture, and a wet house provides plenty. The Texas Department of Insurance says mold can start growing within a day after a leak. A supply line, a failed washer valve or a backed-up condensate line can leave drywall damp for days before anyone sees it.

Fix the leak first. Cleaning mold off a wall that is still getting wet only buys a few weeks. Next, dry the structure quickly. That is the job of water mitigation, which uses air movers, LGR dehumidifiers, moisture meters and thermal imaging to find and dry hidden water.

If mold is already visible, drying alone is not enough, because the growth is still on the material. Crews dry first, then remove what is affected, so the repair does not close in a damp wall. Keep wet items you can move, pull up wet carpet, and photograph the damage before cleanup. Ask the crew to show you the readings.

Mold testing and inspection

Assessment and remediation are separate jobs in Texas. The Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR) licenses mold assessors and mold remediators. Collecting air or visible spore samples counts as assessment, so sampling is not something a remediator does on the same project.

Remediation of 25 contiguous square feet or more of visible mold generally needs a licensed remediator. Smaller areas, under 25 contiguous square feet, may be cleaned by unlicensed people. One licensed company or person generally cannot do both the assessment and the remediation on the same project.

Licensed assessors and remediators must hand you a Consumer Mold Information Sheet before work. Only a licensed remediator can issue a Certificate of Mold Damage Remediation. Use the TDLR Verify a License search to confirm any company you hire.

Clearance testing

After remediation, an independent assessor can check that the work met the plan. This is called clearance testing, and it is what many lenders, buyers and insurers ask to see. Ask the assessor what the report will include before you hire one.

What a mold job looks like from call to certificate

The order matters, and it is why some jobs take longer than expected.

  1. Find the moisture. A technician uses a moisture meter and thermal camera to trace the water, and records readings and photos.
  2. Set the scope. The written scope lists the area, materials to remove and containment needed, and price is explained before work begins.
  3. Contain and filter. Plastic barriers go up and HEPA air filtration runs for the whole job.
  4. Remove and clean. Porous materials come out. Surfaces that can be cleaned are cleaned.
  5. Dry and verify. Moisture readings must be normal before anything is rebuilt, and a licensed remediator issues the certificate.
