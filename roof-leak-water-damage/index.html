Roof leak water damage in Addison starts above the ceiling and often stays hidden until a stain or a drip shows up. Calls are taken through storms and overnight, and same-day response is offered when available.

While you wait, move belongings out from under the leak, catch drips with a bucket and stay out of any room with a bulging ceiling. Do not climb onto a wet roof to look for the source.

How rain gets from the roof to your rooms

Water rarely falls straight through a roof. It enters at a weak point, runs along decking or a rafter, and drops somewhere else. Common entry points are lifted or missing shingles, cracked flashing around a chimney or wall, vent pipe boots, skylight curbs and valleys where two slopes meet.

Once inside, water soaks the attic insulation first. Insulation acts like a sponge and holds water against the decking and the ceiling board beneath it. Framing and the back of the ceiling take on moisture long before paint shows a stain, so the visible spot is usually smaller than the wet area.

Why the stain is not where the leak is

A ceiling stain marks where water came down, not where it came in. Water can travel several feet along a joist before it finds a gap. That is why we map the attic and ceiling with a thermal camera instead of judging by the stain alone.

First-hour safety steps

Water overhead is both a weight and an electrical hazard.

  • Stay out from under a bowed ceiling. It can drop water and board without warning.
  • If water drips through a light fixture or near the panel, switch off that circuit at the breaker only if you can reach it dry. If you cannot tell whether the wiring is safe, stay out and call 911.
  • Do not use ceiling fans or lights that are wet.
  • Do not walk on a wet or hail-damaged roof. Call a roofer to tarp or repair it.
  • Move furniture, electronics and rugs out of the drip zone after photographing them.
  • Call 911 for downed lines, fire or injuries, and the gas utility if you smell gas.

Soaked attic insulation can carry dust and debris. If you must touch it, wear gloves and a mask, and leave the rest to us.

Roofer and restorer do different jobs

A roofer repairs or tarps the roof. We do not do roofing. We work inside, where the water went. Ideally the roof is covered the same day, so the next rain does not undo the drying. Call us and your roofer at the same time, and we will schedule around each other.

How we dry the attic and the ceiling below

Industry practice under ANSI/IICRC S500 is the reference for how the drying is run.

  1. Map the water. A thermal camera and moisture meters trace water from the attic through the ceiling and into walls.
  2. Extract what fell. Portable extractors and truck-mount units remove water from floors and carpet. Pooled water on attic surfaces is pulled up where we can reach it safely.
  3. Remove wet material. Soaked insulation is bagged out. Ceiling board that is soft, sagging or stained through is cut out so the cavity can dry, and we keep the cuts to the wet area.
  4. Dry the cavities. Air movers, LGR dehumidifiers and injectidry cavity drying ports push dry air through ceiling and wall cavities. HEPA air scrubbers clean the air if dust is stirred up.
  5. Monitor and document. We return to read moisture levels against a dry reference reading, and you get photos and readings for your adjuster.

Our structural drying page explains cavity drying in detail, and the overall approach matches our water mitigation work. Once everything is dry, finishing and rebuild belong to water damage repair.

Wear, storms and your policy

Insurers usually separate sudden storm damage from wear. A roof opened by hail or wind during a storm may be treated differently from shingles worn out over years or a leak at old flashing that went unattended. Your own policy language decides, so bring it to your agent or adjuster.

Photograph the ceiling, the attic and any wet contents, and keep receipts for temporary tarping. Note the date of the storm. We add moisture readings and drying logs. Do not throw out wet insulation before your adjuster has seen photos. If water also came through windows or doors in the same storm, see storm water damage.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming the leak is fixed because the dripping stopped.
  • Drying with a household fan and ignoring the attic.
  • Walking on a wet roof to find the source.
