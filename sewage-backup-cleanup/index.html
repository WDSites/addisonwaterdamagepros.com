Why sewage backups are a different kind of water loss

Most water losses begin with a supply line or an appliance, so the water starts clean. A sewage backup does not. The water carries human waste and bacteria, and the industry treats it as Category 3, or grossly contaminated.

That changes the job. Anything porous that the water touched has to be judged on contamination first and moisture second.

How a backup happens

A blockage leaves the wastewater nowhere to go, so it rises to the first opening it finds at the bottom of the house. That is often a floor drain, a ground-level tub, or the toilet nearest the main line. Roots, grease, wipes and collapsed pipe are common causes, and a plumber has to find which one applies.

First-hour safety steps

Treat all backup water as a health hazard until it has been cleaned out.

  • Stop using toilets, sinks, tubs, the dishwasher and the washing machine.
  • Keep children, pets and anyone with a weakened immune system out of the area.
  • Do not wade through it. If you must cross it, wear waterproof boots and rubber gloves, then wash your hands thoroughly.
  • Turn off power to the affected area at the breaker only if you can reach the panel without standing in water.
  • Do not pour bleach on carpet, drywall or other porous material. It does not make those materials safe.
  • Call a plumber to clear the line. Call 911 or the gas utility if you smell gas, and the Town if you suspect the main.

Photograph the water, the fixtures and the damage before anything is moved. Your insurer will want that record.

Our sewage cleanup process

The order of work matters here. We contain first, remove second and dry last.

  1. Protect the crew and the house. Technicians wear gloves, boots, eye protection and respirators. We seal off doorways and vents so contamination does not travel to clean rooms.
  2. Extract the water. Truck-mount or portable extractors pull standing wastewater. We stay on the job until the surface is clear, because the pooled liquid is the main hazard.
  3. Remove porous materials. Carpet, cushion, wet drywall and insulation that touched the water are cut out and bagged. Flood cut tools let us remove board at a clean line above the wick mark.
  4. Clean and treat. Hard surfaces and framing are scrubbed and treated with an antimicrobial product matched to the surface. HEPA air scrubbers filter the air while we work.
  5. Dry the structure. Moisture meters and a thermal camera show where water remains. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the cavities, and structural drying explains how that stage is monitored.
  6. Control odor and document. We address lingering odor after the source is removed, then record readings and photos for your file.

Industry practice under ANSI/IICRC S500 treats contaminated porous material as something to remove, not rescue.

Who handles which part

A licensed plumber clears the blockage, runs a camera if needed and repairs the pipe. We do not offer plumbing repair. We handle the wastewater, the damaged materials and the drying, and the two trades often work the same day.

Insurance and sewage backup

Many standard homeowner policies treat sewer or drain backup separately from other water losses, and it is often an optional endorsement. Policies differ, so ask your agent or adjuster what yours says before you assume either way.

Sudden versus gradual also matters. A sudden backup is judged differently from long-term seepage. Keep photos, receipts and the plumber's report together, and we will give you our readings and notes in a format an adjuster can follow.

What this means in Addison

The Town of Addison buys its water entirely from the City of Dallas, which covers the supply side. For the sewer side, ask the Town or a plumber to find out whether the blockage is on your side.

Plumbing work such as replacing sewer lines needs a permit from Town Development Services, and contractors must register with the Town before starting. The Town runs permits and inspections through its Citizen Self Service portal.

Paint and carpet are cosmetic and need no permit. Wall board replacement is not spelled out on the Town's list, so ask Building Inspection before you rebuild.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Running a household vacuum on the water, which spreads contamination.
  • Keeping wet carpet because it looks fine after cleaning.
  • Leaving cushion and baseboards to dry on their own.
  • Mopping with bleach and calling it done.

If mold shows up later, mold remediation is a separate service. For general cleanup once the structure is safe, see water damage cleanup.
