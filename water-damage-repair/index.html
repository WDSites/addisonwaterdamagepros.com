Water Damage Repair in Addison, TX

Once the structure is dry, we replace what the water ruined: drywall, insulation, flooring, paint, cabinets and trim.

☎ (972) 945-2885

Water damage repair in Addison, TX is the rebuild phase that follows drying. It means cutting out materials that cannot be saved, then installing new drywall, insulation, flooring, cabinets and finish work so the room looks and works as it did before.

Repair starts only after moisture readings show the structure is dry. Closing up wet framing is how a small leak turns into a mold job six weeks later.

Ceiling and wall water damage repair

A sagging ceiling, a brown stain ring, or drywall that feels soft under your thumb all tell you the board has absorbed water. Gypsum board loses strength when it stays wet, and a ceiling holding water can drop without warning. If it bulges, keep people out from under it and ask a technician to release the water safely.

The source matters. An upstairs supply line, a failed shower pan or a roof leak each leave a different pattern, and the leak has to be fixed by a plumber or roofer before any new board goes up. Those are separate trades, and we will tell you plainly when the next call is theirs.

What gets cut out depends on moisture mapping. Wet or stained board is removed, usually in clean cuts of 2 to 4 feet so the new piece lands on framing. A small dry-edged stain can sometimes be sealed and patched, but soft, sagging or repeatedly wet board should be replaced. Wet batt insulation above the ceiling is pulled too, since it holds water and stops drying.

Texture matching matters on open ceilings and walls under raking light. Knockdown, orange peel and skip trowel each need a matching technique, and a whole wall is often repainted so the patch does not show.

Hardwood floor cupping after a washer, HVAC or supply line leak

Cupping happens when the bottom of a board absorbs more moisture than the top, so the edges rise and the middle dips. A slow leak from a washer valve, an HVAC condensate line or a braided supply line can cause it before you see any puddle.

Many floors can be saved if the water source is stopped early and the boards are dried in place with controlled airflow, dehumidification and, sometimes, drying mats. Drying takes days, not hours, and cupping can keep changing during that time. Sanding too soon can leave the floor flat for a month and crowned after it dries out fully.

Some boards cannot be saved. Wet floors that have buckled off the subfloor, delaminated engineered planks, and boards soaked for weeks usually need replacement, and so does flooring over a subfloor that stayed wet. We will not promise a result before a moisture reading of the wood and subfloor. After drying, we check again and tell you whether to sand and refinish or pull the boards and install new ones.

From drying sign-off to rebuild

Repair follows a set order so nothing gets covered before it is ready.

  1. Confirm dry readings. A moisture meter checks framing, subfloor and drywall against the drying goal for that material.
  2. Demolition and cleanup. Remaining wet or damaged material is removed, and dust is contained with plastic and negative air where needed.
  3. Insulation, board and mud. New insulation goes in, then drywall, taping and finish coats with dry time between each.
  4. Texture, prime and paint. Texture is matched, stain-blocking primer goes over old rings, and walls are painted corner to corner.
  5. Flooring, cabinets and trim. Floors, base cabinets, baseboards and casing go back last so they are not damaged by earlier work.

How scope and price are explained before work starts

Before any rebuild begins, a technician inspects the damage, records moisture readings, takes photos and writes the scope in plain language. You see what is being replaced, what is being repaired and why, and you hear the price before work starts. If something changes, such as hidden rot behind a vanity, you get the change explained first.

Texas homeowners should know that repairs may need a permit. The Town of Addison does not require one for cosmetic work like painting, carpeting, cabinets and trim, but plumbing work, foundation repair and removing or adding walls do. Rules for drywall and general flooring are not spelled out, so ask the Town's Building Inspection staff whether your repair needs a permit.

Insurance note: sudden and accidental water damage, like a burst pipe, is often covered, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not. What we do not repair

We rebuild what water damaged. We do not fix the source. Roof repair and plumbing repair are separate trades, and an unrepaired leak will damage the new work too. Restoration is also not waterproofing, so a basement that floods after rain needs a drainage fix, not just new drywall.

If your home is still wet, start with water mitigation. If debris, damaged contents or odor remain, water damage cleanup comes first. We also repair homes near Addison, including Carrollton.
